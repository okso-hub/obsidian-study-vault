# Intro & the KDD process

> [!question] Guiding questions
> - What distinguishes data mining from classical statistics?
> - What are the phases of the KDD process, and why is preprocessing usually the largest?
> - What is the difference between descriptive and predictive tasks?

> [!note] Notes
> - **Data mining** = extracting patterns from large datasets. Sits inside the broader **KDD** (Knowledge Discovery in Databases) process.
> - KDD phases: *selection → preprocessing → transformation → mining → evaluation → interpretation*. Preprocessing is famously ~60–80% of effort in practice.
> - **Descriptive** tasks: clustering, association rules — find structure. **Predictive** tasks: classification, regression — predict a target.
> - Contrast with statistics: DM tolerates weaker theoretical guarantees for scale; stats emphasises inference and uncertainty. In practice the border is blurry.
> - CRISP-DM as an industry-oriented alternative to KDD: adds a *business understanding* and *deployment* phase.

> [!summary] Summary (in your own words, max. 5 sentences)
> Data mining is the pattern-extraction step inside the larger KDD/CRISP-DM process, sitting between preprocessing and evaluation. Most real-world effort is in preprocessing, not modelling. Tasks split into descriptive (find structure) and predictive (predict a target). Data mining trades statistical rigor for scale and pattern-finding power. In this course we follow the KDD framing.
